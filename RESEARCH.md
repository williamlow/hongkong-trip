# Research notes

Checked on 31 October 2026 (Hong Kong time). This is a desk-researched personal guide, not a record of meals eaten or a guarantee of present-day operation.

## Scope and selection

Read Eater’s 38-restaurant city guide (updated 23 December 2025), The Infatuation’s broad 25-place guide and its March 2025 individual reviews, MICHELIN’s 2026 listings, and Sassy guides to local cafes, Jordan/Yau Ma Tei, dim sum, claypot rice, dai pai dongs, budget dining and Sham Shui Po. Use OpenRice for branch/address/status checks and the Hong Kong Tourism Board for neighbourhood context.

The final 48 stops have 63 citation links to 52 unique pages from five publishers/directories. Tourism-board pages are background only and do not imply endorsement of individual restaurants. The shortlist filters out international restaurants, formal dining and most expensive venues. Cha chaan teng breakfasts count as local Hong Kong food despite their historical Western influences.

Eater’s dollar bands are broad (its cheapest band can include dishes under HK$300), so they were not treated as a precise cheap-meal budget. All displayed ranges are independently labelled planning estimates for a typical individual order, snack or per-person shared meal. They are not transcribed menu prices. Oi Man Sing is explicitly an optional bigger-bill shared dinner.

## Corrections and exclusions

- Kai Kai: MICHELIN says it moved in 2025. Use **113–115 Parkes Street**, not the older Ning Po Street address.
- Wang Fu: the current MICHELIN listing says **168 Wellington Street**. Older Sassy coverage lists 65 Wellington Street, which was not carried forward.
- Keung Kee: use MICHELIN’s current **406 Lockhart Road** listing.
- Lau Sum Kee: selected MICHELIN’s **82 Fuk Wing Street** branch. Eater lists number 80; the discrepancy is disclosed on the card. Do not mix this pin with the Kweilin Street original.
- Gold Garden: the review is categorised as Mong Kok, but **314 Castle Peak Road** is in Cheung Sha Wan. The guide uses the address-based neighbourhood.
- One Dim Sum: use the **209A–209B Tung Choi Street** branch, confirmed by OpenRice, rather than a similarly named shop.
- Four Seasons and Sheung Hei: Sassy’s specialist claypot article is from 2023. Cross-checked branch listings in OpenRice; the age of the editorial source remains visible on each card.
- Hotel coordinates and address use Eaton HK’s official website metadata. Most restaurant pins use the publication/directory’s branch coordinates. Three street positions (Australia Dairy Company, Hing Kee and Sing Heung Yuen) remain approximate; all map pins are labelled approximate and Google Maps searches use names plus branch addresses.
- Tai On Coffee & Tea was dropped: Eater describes a modern cafe/cocktail concept with fusion dishes, a weaker match for the requested traditional local character.
- Tung Po was dropped from the draft to keep the overall shortlist lower-budget. Oi Man Sing supplies the clearly labelled optional shared-plate dinner.
- Several initial general-knowledge candidates were replaced rather than presented without supporting current branch evidence. The Infatuation’s 2016 broad guide was useful for discovery but was not used as sole evidence of present-day operation.

An accessible restaurant listing is evidence of a published listing, not proof of a door being open today. OpenRice’s “open”/“closed now” labels must not be confused with permanent closure. Menus, portions, prices and operating days change. The guide links every branch to Google Maps and advises a recent-activity/opening check before travelling. A few useful closing-day notes explicitly identify their source and remain subject to rechecking.

Walks are rough estimates: great-circle distance × 1.35, at 4.5 km/h. “Near Eaton” is a distance filter of approximately 1 km in a straight line. It is not live routing or an accessibility assessment.

Only brief independently written summaries are reproduced; no restaurant photographs or long review passages were copied.

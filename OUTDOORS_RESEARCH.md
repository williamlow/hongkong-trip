# Rural day trips and hikes for the heat

Research checked **3 November 2026, Hong Kong time**. The user missed a weekday Lai Chi Wo ferry and wanted comparable day activities plus a separate hiking page. Their hiking constraints were shorter walks, shade and/or downhill, with a preference for countryside and willingness to travel. Existing preference for casual Chinese/Hong Kong food was retained.

## Evaluation approach

This update compares the complete outing: transport, road approaches, climbing, sun gaps, food, early turnaround and the return connection. It does not treat “easy”, “short” or “shaded” as proof of suitability in severe heat. The user's observed heat is the planning constraint; no cause or ENSO attribution is asserted.

Twenty-three unique Tourism Board and Sassy source URLs in `outdoorSources` were successfully fetched. Relevant article bodies were read, and older publication dates/editorial notes inspected. The current environment blocks the Observatory, Transport Department and AFCD hosts with proxy HTTP 403. Consequently **live weather warnings, trail closures, actual sailings, capacity, fares and admission availability are unverified**. Published service patterns are labelled as leads, and current-check links are kept separate from the sources read. No booking, ferry purchase, permit or third-party message was made.

### Lai Chi Wo lead

The [Tourism Board Lai Chi Wo page](https://www.discoverhongkong.com/eng/place-to-go/travel.guide-lai-chi-wo.html) describes:

- Shatin Area 77 Landing near Tai Shui Hang: Tuesdays/Thursdays, except public holidays.
- Ma Liu Shui: mainly Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays.
- Sha Tau Kok: a separate access route requiring a closed-area permit.

The site prominently labels this **“Published lead · current sailings unconfirmed”**. It does not promise the next Thursday service, copy a Ma Liu Shui departure time to Area 77, or suggest casually entering the restricted pier. Users are told to confirm route, exact pier, both sailing times, their date and booking/capacity before leaving Eaton.

Distances are explicitly labelled as published cores or **planning estimates for adaptations**, not GPS measurements. No invented cumulative ascent totals or shade percentages are supplied. There is no GPX/KML or drawn hiking line: the map shows approximate access references and a selected finish. Google Maps search/transit/driving links do not certify a walking route through country parks.

### Options evaluated and left out

- Exposed ridges/beach routes such as Dragon's Back and High Junk Peak have sun/climbing/exit disadvantages for this brief.
- The shaded-hikes article itself describes major climbing, steps or uneven ground for High West, Razor Hill and Ng Tung Chai. Its “shaded” label was not accepted as a low-effort recommendation. Stream/waterfall cooling and swimming were not carried into these plans.
- Full Shing Mun and the approximately 12 km family route into Lai Chi Wo are different commitments from the short returns. A missed boat does not make the long approach appropriate.
- Hok Tau is described as a quieter wooded reservoir, but the additional routes checked at Hok Tau/Wu Kau Tang involve longer approaches or major extensions. They were not promoted as verified short, shaded door-to-door outings without better access evidence.
- Tung Ping Chau and Kat O are not interchangeable spontaneous weekday ferry replacements. The site does not invent current weekday departures for them.

## Map and software validation

Tourism Board destination coordinates were combined with inspected OpenStreetMap tiles and matched Nominatim results. Rejected unrelated Hoi Ha geocoder results in Kwai Chung/other districts and the Tai Po Kau reserve centroid as an entrance. The Hoi Ha pin is the village by the road, not the marine-park bay centroid; its Maps query also names the village. Parkview's reference is outside the private estate at the public-road approach. Pok Fu Lam and Tai Tam finishes refer to lower public-road access, and Braemar Hill to its transport-terminus area. All remain approximate, with actual access/closures to confirm.

The two static pages are generated by `scripts/outdoor-pages.mjs` from `outdoors-data.js` and committed for Pages root deployment. Filters keep details cards, marker counts and result text in sync. Comparison links restore a hidden card, update its fragment and preserve keyboard focus. Selected one-way hikes add a separately labelled finish; no line implies a surveyed route. The shared six-tab navigation wraps into a three-column grid on small phones.

Tests cover data/citation/access integrity, planning-category distinctions, all section links, filter/map sync, comparison navigation and focus, start/finish queries, high-start taxi routing, explicit ferry/weather uncertainty, no-JS content, blocked tiles, mobile/tablet overflow and map refitting. Desktop/phone screenshots use real OSM tiles through the normal verified HTTPS proxy. These checks validate the software, not the safety or present operation of any outing.
